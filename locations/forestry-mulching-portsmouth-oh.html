  • Questions We Get Every Week

    • What is forestry mulching? One tracked machine grinds standing brush, briars, and small trees into chips right where they stand. Nothing is burned, piled, or hauled off the property.
    • How do you price a job? By the acre or by the day, whichever fits the ground better. Stem size, slope, and access move the number far more than total acreage does.
    • Are you licensed and insured? Yes, and we send a certificate of insurance before the machine leaves the shop. Ask any contractor for one — hesitation tells you something.
    • Is brush hogging better than mulching? A brush hog cuts soft growth a few inches off the ground and leaves the woody stems behind. A mulcher takes stems up to eight or ten inches and grinds them flush.
    • Do you remove stumps? Mulching cuts at the stump and leaves the root mat holding the ground together. If you need bare, buildable dirt, that is land clearing and it prices separately.
    • How soon can you start? Most quotes go out within 24 hours, and scheduling usually runs a couple of weeks out. Storm work jumps the queue.
    • Do you haul the debris away? With mulching, no — the material stays on site as ground cover, and that is most of the savings. Junk, scrap, and tires are a property cleanup job.
    • What do you need from me first? Marked property lines, a way in for a truck and trailer, and anything you want left standing flagged. Tell us about septic lines, wells, and buried anything.
    • What areas do you serve? Southern Ohio and Eastern Kentucky along the Ohio River corridor. We take work a county or two past the map regularly — ask anyway.

Forestry Mulching in Portsmouth, OH

Scioto County hands you two completely different clearing jobs, sometimes on the same farm: deep river bottom that grows brush faster than you can cut it, and hillside too steep to put a tractor on.

Portsmouth sits where the Scioto empties into the Ohio, and that confluence is the whole story of the ground here. North and west of town the bottoms are flat, silt-deep, and absurdly fertile — which is exactly why an unworked field there goes to box elder, sycamore, and giant ragweed in three seasons. Behind town the hills go up at an angle no bush hog will ever see.

Forestry mulching suits both, for different reasons. In the bottoms it means you are not stacking and burning a decade of willow and cottonwood. On the hills it means the root mat stays intact on ground that would otherwise start moving. Either way the material stays on your property as a chip layer instead of leaving on a truck.

If the goal is a building pad, a pond bank, or driveway cut down to soil, that is land clearing. If a spring storm put timber across your access, start with storm cleanup.

The Land Around Portsmouth

River Bottom and Hillside Are Two Different Jobs

The Scioto and Ohio bottoms grow vegetation at a rate that surprises people who move here. Silt loam, a high water table, and full sun mean a field left alone for four or five years is genuinely impassable. The species are predictable — box elder, silver maple, sycamore, black willow, and enough giant ragweed to hide a truck.

Behind that, the unglaciated hills start immediately: Sciotoville, New Boston, and the ground running west toward Shawnee State Forest. Those slopes are second-growth hardwood over sandstone and shale, with greenbrier and multiflora rose filling every gap in the canopy. Same county, same customer sometimes, and nothing about the two jobs is alike.

Bottoms Hold Water Late

A wet spring keeps the Scioto bottoms soft into June. We would rather reschedule than leave ruts in a field you farm. Winter, once the ground firms, is the best window down there.

Fast, Soft Regrowth

Bottomland species are fast but soft. Mulching eats box elder and willow quickly, so bottom acreage per day usually runs well ahead of what the same acreage on a hillside would.

Hills Right Behind Town

Scioto County slopes are short and steep rather than long and gradual. Tracks handle them, but we plan the cut so the machine is working across the slope instead of up and down it.

What Scioto County Landowners Call Us About

A Bottom Field Gone to Brush

Five years out of production and the field is a thicket. Mulching puts it back to workable ground in a pass, with the chips tilling in rather than burning in a pile at the field edge.

The Hillside Behind the House

Too steep to mow, too close to ignore. Mulching thins the understory and opens the view while the mature trees holding the bank stay exactly where they are.

Ground Next to Shawnee State Forest

Private tracts against the forest boundary grow shut from the edge in. Mark the line and we work to it — we do not guess at a boundary we cannot see.

Fence Rows Eating the Field

Every year the row creeps another few feet. Reclaiming a fence line on a Scioto County farm often gives back more tillable acres than people expect.

Portsmouth Mulching Questions

It depends far more on which half of the county you are on than on total acreage. Flat bottom ground with soft, fast-growing species covers more acres per day than a hillside of hardwood saplings and greenbrier, and the price follows that directly. Send photos from ground level plus a rough acreage and you will get a real number back, normally inside 24 hours.

When a tracked machine starts leaving a rut instead of a track. In the Scioto and Ohio bottoms that is most of a wet spring. We will tell you when to wait — a job pushed to a firm week in winter costs the same and leaves your field in far better shape than one forced in April.

Late fall through winter, for four reasons: the leaves are down so the operator can actually see the ground, the stems are dormant, the bottoms are firm, and there are no ticks or snakes in the brush. Summer work is perfectly doable — it is just slower going and harder on everyone.

We can work up to your property line, and we need you to show us where it is. Flagging, a survey pin, or an existing fence is enough. We will not cut toward a boundary that is only marked on a plat in somebody’s file cabinet, because the machine does not un-cut anything.
